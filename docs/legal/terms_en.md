## 2. What the app is — and is not

- Fakkarni reminds you to take medicines at the times **you** set, and can
  alert people you choose when a dose is not confirmed.
- **It is not a medical device and does not give medical advice.** It does not
  diagnose, recommend, change or stop any medicine. Default timings (for
  example 30 minutes before a meal) are editable conventions, not clinical
  guidance.
- **Readings from photos** (prescriptions, lab reports, medicine boxes) are
  produced by an automated system and **can be wrong**. Nothing is saved or
  scheduled until you review and confirm it. You are responsible for checking
  what you confirm. When unsure, ask your pharmacist or doctor.
- Measurements and "your usual" figures are your own numbers compared with
  each other; they are not medical ranges.

## 3. Reminders can fail

Phones can delay or block notifications (battery saving, silent mode, Focus,
permissions turned off, the phone switched off, no internet for alerts to
family). **Do not rely on Fakkarni as the only way to take a medicine that is
critical to your health.** Alerts to family members depend on the patient's
phone syncing and on their phone receiving notifications, and can arrive late
or not at all.

## 4. Accounts and the care circle

- You can use the app without an account. An account is created only when you
  link a family member or nurse.
- The patient decides who is linked and what they may do (see, confirm doses
  on their behalf, request medicine changes), and can remove anyone at any
  time.
- A follower or nurse must only link to a patient who agreed to it, and must
  use the patient's data only to help the patient.
- Keep your invite codes private. A code is valid for 15 minutes and once.
